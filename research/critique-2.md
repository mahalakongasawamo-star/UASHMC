# 1. DOES IT CLOSE?

## Where it fails to drive AGREEMENT

**1.1 The commercial vacuum.** `delivery-plan.md` A1 item 7 says: _"Signed SOW for Phase 1, and the ongoing maintenance retainer... **P0 — blocks start**. No fallback. Work does not start on a verbal."_ Correct — and there is no SOW, no fee, no payment schedule, no retainer figure, and no KRISS price anywhere in the package. You have written the gate and not built the key.

Worse, the two-path slide ("Fast path Week 8 / Full scope Week 17") presents an unpriced binary choice. An executive cannot choose between two things with no cost attached. The recommendation _is_ in the document — _"Take the fast path as Phase 1"_ — but it is buried under the comparison table and hedged. Executives choose from a recommendation, not a menu.

**Fix:** one page. Phase 1 fee, three-tranche payment schedule with mobilization on signature, retainer/month, KRISS setup + monthly, what's excluded. Put your recommendation at the top in bold and give the price for the recommended path first.

**1.2 There is no low-friction first yes.** The close in section F is nine verbal asks. Verbal commitments in a room with five senior stakeholders evaporate in the car park. Nothing in the material offers Dr. Bravo a small, cheap, immediate decision.

**Fix — and this is the single highest-leverage change:** offer a **paid two-week mobilization sprint** at a fixed, modest fee that starts Monday while the full SOW is papered. It buys: domain secured, GBP claimed and populated, NAP corrected across Waze/GBP/NowServing/HealthSpace, content packs issued, photo shoot scheduled, the five blocking documents chased. All of that is real work, all of it is useful even if Phase 1 never happens, and it converts "we'll get back to you" into a client. A big yes is hard. A small yes with a receipt is easy, and it makes the big yes procedural.

**1.3 The nine asks are asymmetric.** Read section F again: name an owner, book a call, book a shoot, settle the domain, return content in 10 days, approve a template, get the LTO, choose a path, adopt working rules. **Every one is work for them.** There is no reciprocal, dated commitment from you.

**Fix:** add a "What we will have done by Friday" column beside the nine. Domain registered. GBP claimed. Content packs in their inbox. Release form drafted. Decision log live. Symmetry is what makes a list of demands read as a partnership.

**1.4 No value hypothesis, so the price has no anchor.** Q2 asks _"Twelve months after this site is live, what has changed that makes you say it was worth it?"_ — excellent discovery, and you must not arrive with only the question. Propose numbers and invite correction: expected organic sessions, NowServing click-throughs as the census proxy, tel: taps, GBP calls and direction requests, after-hours share. Being wrong and correctable beats having no view. Without a value hypothesis, whatever price you name is a cost, not an investment.

**1.5 No risk reversal.** The 5-working-day default-approval rule protects _your_ schedule. Nothing protects theirs. A first-time buyer with no reference site to check needs one asymmetric commitment from you — a launch-date guarantee with a stated consequence, a fixed-fee cap, or a milestone exit at design approval. Pick one and offer it before they ask for it.

**1.6 The honest-limitations slide is right and mispositioned.** Twelve limitations delivered to a board _before_ agreement in principle converts diligence into doubt. `kriss-integration.md` §10 has twelve rows. **Three in the room, twelve in the pack.** The three: it only knows what you publish; it does not diagnose, ever, by design; you would be the first hospital deployment.

**1.7 Missing entirely: what the strategic claim is worth.** _"First Philippine hospital with a real on-site patient-navigation assistant"_ is the strongest asset in the package and it is used only as a timeline argument (_"That claim has a shelf life"_). Make it the close. Bravo is a competitive man running a hospital nobody has heard of; the chance to be first in the country at something verifiable is the emotional reason he signs. Put it last, put a date on it, and put a price under it.

# 2. THE HARVEY PROBLEM

**Verdict: the machinery for including him exists. The proportions insult him.**

The `OPEN DECISION` pins, the Content-sources toggle, Q7, and the four named decisions in `sitemap.md` §8 are real and genuinely better than what most agencies do. And they will not survive contact with a Marketing Director who reads what has already been settled.

## Where he gets steamrolled — precisely

- _"concern-first (**recommended**)"_
- _"Call, Book, Ask is the **recommendation**; Directions is the credible fourth contender"_

Defensible design call; terrible thing to arrive pre-decided. Same with §2.2's _"their brand blue may fail 4.5:1 on white and need a darkened text variant"_ — you are telling him, in writing, that his brand colour fails.

**Fix:** put the tagline in the hero in one of your two directions. Let him choose to demote it. If he demotes it himself, it is a design insight; if you demote it, it is a slight.

**2.4 "Iozera drafts, UASHMC approves" is exactly wrong for him.** `delivery-plan.md` B0 makes it the governing principle: _"We do not ask UASHMC to author content. We ask them to correct ours."_ That is right for Tere and Dr. Pedraja, who have day jobs. To a Marketing Director it reads as _we write the marketing, you proofread it._

**Fix — say this sentence in the room:** _"That principle applies to clinical and operational content, where we are drafting to save your people time. It does not apply to brand, voice and campaign copy. There, you direct and we execute."_ Carve him out explicitly and audibly.

**2.5 He is the only stakeholder with no artifact.** Bravo gets the licensing block and the price decision. Pedraja gets a **signed** guardrail spec with her name on it. Tere gets the owner table and the content packs. Harvey gets markers he can click.

**Fix — give him two named, dated deliverables at his level of seniority:**

- **The Brand & Voice Sheet.** Currently buried as Iozera deliverable #67 and #11 (_"Iozera produces a mini web brand sheet"_). Reframe it: **his** document, drafted to his direction, **he** signs it, and it governs everything downstream **including KRISS's tone of voice.** That hands him authorship of the layer that contains all the other layers.
- **The photography and launch workstream.** Shot list, shoot date, launch announcement, GBP population. His name on the timeline.

Then mention the **New Service Spotlight** component (`sitemap.md` §4) early rather than burying it: a homepage slot he publishes into _without calling a developer_. That is a power tool and it is the thing that makes him want this project to exist.

**2.6 Present two homepage directions, not one.** Cheapest high-leverage fix available. Even a second hero treatment and a second section order — a warmer, photo-led hero versus the utility-first task console — turns "approve or reject our design" into "pick a direction with me." A Marketing Director who chooses between two things you built feels like a client. One shown a single finished thing feels like an audience.

**2.7 Reorder the demo so he co-authors the task grid retroactively.** Ask Q7 — _what are the top twenty questions people actually ask on Messenger_ — **before** you reveal the task grid. Then when the grid appears and four of his answers are on it, he has co-written it and he will defend it against his own colleagues. If the grid comes first, his only available move is critique. **This single reordering is worth more than every OPEN DECISION pin combined.**

**2.8 One table to move to the appendix.** `homepage-spec.md` §1.4 lists seven brochure elements under "Why it fails for UASHMC" — stock photography, mission/vision, President's message, awards carousel, testimonials. Several of those are things a Marketing Director would normally ask for. Presented as-is it reads as _here are the seven dumb things you were going to ask for._ Either move it out of the room or reframe every row as _"a choice we made and would defend — argue with us."_

# 4. COMMERCIAL OMISSIONS

Ranked by what a real deal cannot proceed without.

**1. Price — absent entirely.** Covered in §1. Non-negotiable.

**2. KRISS commercial model — absent, and actively dangerous.** Your own Appendix A #7: _"KRISS's public price is $199/month plus a $500 setup fee — SMB dental-clinic pricing that UASHMC can find in thirty seconds. Decide how a hospital engagement is framed before someone anchors on it."_ That decision has not been made anywhere in the package.

Needs: configuration/setup fee, monthly, what the monthly buys (corpus sync, guardrail regression testing, monthly unanswered-query report, **Dr. Pedraja's clinical log review**), who pays for model inference, and behaviour at volume. **Price it as a governed clinical deployment, never as the SaaS number.** And offer a **90-day pilot at reduced rate with a written exit** — that is how you sell an unproven product to a risk-averse clinical buyer.

**3. SEO and Google Business Profile — the most surprising gap given the sophistication elsewhere.** A brand-new hospital with no domain is invisible. The material has real SEO thinking scattered through it — schema.org, semantic HTML, stable URLs, AI answer engines — and yet:

- **Not one target query is named anywhere.** "hospital antipolo", "cardiologist antipolo", "emergency room marcos highway", "maxicare hospital antipolo", "hospital near masinag", "gallbladder surgery antipolo". These are the queries that produce census and nobody has written them down.

**Fix: one page.** Ten target queries, a GBP workstream with an owner and a two-week timeline, citation cleanup, and a 90-day/6-month expectation. This is also a legitimate retained service you are currently giving away.

**4. Analytics and website success measurement — badly underweight.** The KRISS dashboard spec runs to five tabs and forty fields. The _website_ gets _"analytics configured"_ and _"Search Console verification."_ No KPIs, no baseline, no targets, no reporting cadence, no dashboard. Q2 asks the client what success means and you never propose an answer.

**Fix: six KPIs with 6- and 12-month targets.** Organic sessions; **NowServing click-throughs** (your best available census proxy); **tel: taps** (the real conversion event on a hospital site and trivially instrumented); directory→profile→book funnel completion; GBP calls and direction requests; after-hours traffic share. Then a monthly one-page report inside the retainer. **Without this the retainer is unsellable at renewal month four.** Choose a privacy-preserving analytics tool and say so — it is consistent with your DPA posture and it is a differentiator.

IP, content and design-file ownership — completely absent.** Nowhere in the package is there a statement of who owns the source code, the design files, the content, or — critically — **the photography copyright.** By default the photographer owns the images. Physician releases cover likeness; they do not assign copyright. A hospital that has paid for a shoot and cannot use the photos in print is a real dispute.

**Fix:** IP assigned to UASHMC on final payment; photography copyright assigned to UASHMC; Iozera retains a portfolio licence (ask for it — see §3.7E); repository and deployment runbook handed over on exit.

**6. Change control — one mechanism, covering one thing.** _"Two review rounds are included; a third is a change order"_ is the only change control in the package and it governs design rounds only. Nothing on scope additions, no change-order rate, no content-volume assumption. **Add:** "Phase 1 includes N service pages and M doctor profiles; additional pages at X each." When the roster goes from 20 doctors to 45 — and it will — you need that line to exist already.

**7. Domain and hosting — well handled on ownership, silent on money and exit.** A1 #3 and R5 are good: registrar in UASHMC's name, transfer letter, account on a UASHMC mailbox. Missing: **who pays for renewals, hosting, CDN, SSL, monitoring** — retainer or pass-through? And **what happens if they leave.** Answer it before they ask: you own the domain, the content, the files; here is the runbook. Volunteered, that is a trust win.

**8. Payment terms, deposit, invoicing.** For a first engagement, a mobilization payment on signature is standard and it is also your closing instrument. 40/30/30 or 50/50.

**9. Liability, insurance, limitation of liability.** A hospital's procurement will ask. The PIC/PIP position is well covered; the liability cap and indemnity are not.

**10. Retainer terms.** D4 is your best commercial section — platform retainer, content allowance, KRISS service, QBR, a proposed 4-business-hour P1 SLA. Still unpriced, and missing term length, notice period, annual uplift, and what happens to the live site if the retainer lapses.

# 6. WHAT COULD GO WRONG IN THE ROOM

## The three hardest questions

### Q1 — Dr. Bravo, or Dr. Directo for the board: _"What does this cost, and what do we get?"_

**Probability: certain. Likely inside the first twenty minutes.** This is the highest-probability failure mode of the entire meeting and the package has no answer.

**The answer must be fluent, not derived on the spot:** Phase 1 is [X] fixed, in three payments — mobilization on signature, [Y] at design approval, [Z] at launch. The retainer is [R] monthly and covers hosting, monitoring, the 4-hour P1 SLA and a content allowance. KRISS is [S] to configure and [T] monthly, and that monthly includes guardrail regression testing and Dr. Pedraja's clinical log review — which is the part that makes it defensible to your board. And here is the value hypothesis: [named metrics].

If you cannot say that without looking down, either fix it tonight or open the meeting with _"commercials at the end, and I will give you real numbers"_ — and then actually give them. **Do not let the meeting end with the number still unnamed.**

### Q2 — Dr. Pedraja: _"Show me another hospital where this chatbot is running."_

There is not one. `kriss.ai`'s own hospital client category is empty; the deployments are US dental practices. The material knows this and answers _"governance substitutes for reference logos"_ — which is right and not sufficient alone.

**Three-part answer, and part one must be volunteered before she asks:**

1. _"There is no hospital deployment anywhere. You would be the first, and I would rather you heard that from me than found it."_
2. Substitute the artifacts, and **hand her the physical printed document at that exact moment**: signed guardrail spec, signed red-flag lexicon, signed routing table, PIA, monthly log review to her desk. _"Governance is better currency than a borrowed logo, and for a hospital board I think you will agree."_
3. **De-risk it structurally — this is missing from the package and it is what actually converts a cautious clinician: a 90-day internal-only pilot, staff-facing, no public exposure, with her holding a kill switch and a written exit.**

And pre-empt the ibuprofen problem in the same breath: _"If you open kriss.ai you will find their demo assistant volunteering ibuprofen advice. That is their dental product. This is the configuration we are proposing for you, and here it is in writing."_ Say it before she finds it.

### Q3 — Harvey, possibly phrased politely: _"You have already designed our website. What am I contributing?"_

Or the sharper operational version, which is worse: _"who writes the copy?"_

**Answer, in four parts:**

1. Name the boundary out loud: _"We drafted so you would have something to react to, not to lock it. Anything on this screen can change. The brand layer is yours, not ours — and clinical drafting is us saving your Medical Director time, not us taking your job."_
2. Hand him the **Brand & Voice Sheet** as his signed artifact — governing the site _and_ KRISS's tone.
3. Give him **two homepage directions to choose between**, not one to approve.
4. If time allows, the real concession: **offer to rebuild the hero to his direction inside 48 hours, and mean it.** A fast, visible response to his first instruction is what converts a Marketing Director from a gatekeeper into a sponsor.
