# A) CONTENT REQUIREMENTS CHECKLIST

**How to read this.** `P0` = blocks launch of the whole site. `P1` = blocks launch of _that module only_ (site can go live without it, module ships later). `P2` = post-launch enrichment. Every P0 item has a fallback, because "we don't have it" must never mean "we don't launch."

**The publish gate that governs everything below:** no service, capability or claim goes on the site unless it appears on UASHMC's DOH License to Operate. That gate is a required field in the CMS, not a policy memo. (RA 10932 Sec. 3 already requires the classification level and authorized services to be posted at the physical entrance — the homepage is now the front door.)

## A6. THE DOMAIN AND EMAIL SITUATION — raise this first, not last

UASHMC's public contact address today is **`uashmc.customerrelations@gmail.com`**.

**Say this plainly in the room, and lead with the security framing, not the branding one:**

1. **It is a credibility leak.** A free consumer mailbox on a hospital's public contact page signals _provisional_ — to a patient deciding whether to trust this institution with surgery. It quietly undercuts everything else on the page, including the DOH licence and the specialist roster.
2. **It is a data-security finding, not a marketing preference.** Patient inquiries containing health information — sensitive personal information under RA 10173 — flow into a consumer mailbox sitting outside any access-control regime the hospital can evidence under **NPC Circular 2023-06** (in force since March 2024, compliance required by March 2025). That reframes the migration from a nice-to-have into a remediation item, which is a far easier budget conversation.
3. **It is the cheapest large trust win available** and it is not a design decision. It costs almost nothing and it forces the domain decision early — which the website needs anyway.
4. **It forces the DNS conversation on day one**, which is the item most likely to silently delay launch by a week (see §C R5).

**The ask, phrased for the room:** _"Before we leave today, can we agree the domain and name one person who will hold the registrar account? We'll provision `customerrelations@`, `info@`, `careers@` and a DPO mailbox, publish the new address on the site, and keep the Gmail forwarding for 90 days so nothing is lost."_

# B) DEVELOPMENT PROCESS + TIMELINE

## B0. The honest framing to open with

Design and development are **five weeks of known, controllable work**. Content approval is the variable, and in hospitals it is _always_ the critical path — because the people who must approve clinical content are clinicians and administrators with day jobs, and because a hospital correctly treats published claims as institutional commitments.

So the plan is built around one structural choice: **Iozera drafts, UASHMC approves.** We do not ask UASHMC to author content. We ask them to correct ours. That single inversion is worth 4–6 weeks.

And one publishing philosophy: **publish thin, then enrich.** The site goes live with fewer pages, each complete. It does not wait for the full catalogue.

### R0 (pre-meeting, ours not theirs) — Iozera's own credibility surface

**The risk.** Harvey Lozada will google us. `iozera.ai/contact/` currently publishes a stock WordPress placeholder address ("198 West 21th Street, Suite 721 New York, NY 10010"), `youremail@yourdomain.com`, a `+88 (0) 101 0000 000` phone, and a blog of untouched theme demo posts including "hello-world." **Mitigation, before the onsite.** Either fix `/contact/` and purge the demo posts, **or** make a deliberate decision never to reference iozera.ai and lead entirely with kriss.ai as the craft proof (record a screen capture as backup — its WebGL preloader stalls). Also settle internally, before anyone speaks: **is KRISS Iozera's product, a sister company, or a client?** iozera.ai currently lists kriss.ai as a _customer testimonial_ while kriss.ai's own site never mentions Iozera. Telling both stories is how we lose Harvey.

---

### R1 — Content approval latency with no single accountable owner

**Rank: #1, and it is not close.** This is the risk that turns an 8-week project into a 6-month one. It presents as "we're just waiting on a few things" for three months.

**Why it happens here specifically:** five senior stakeholders, no named owner, clinical content requiring a Medical Director's signature, financial content requiring Billing, and a brand-new hospital where every operational answer is still being formalised internally.

**The five-part mitigation to propose, by name, in the meeting:**

1. **A named UASHMC project owner with decision authority** — one person, named today, with the mandate to consolidate internal feedback and say yes. Tere Barcelona is the natural fit as the internal driver, with a **content steward** in Harvey's team doing the legwork.
2. **A weekly 30-minute standing call**, same day, same time, in everyone's calendar before we leave. Thirty minutes, not sixty — short enough that it never gets cancelled. Agenda is fixed: what shipped, what's blocked, what's decided.
3. **"Publish thin, then enrich."** The launch cohort is defined by completeness, not ambition. A page is either complete and live or absent — never live and half-empty. This removes the single most common reason to delay a launch.
4. **Pre-approved content templates.** Dr. Pedraja approves the _physician profile template_ and the _service page template_ **once**. Thereafter individual profiles and pages are approved **by exception** — only deltas get reviewed. This is the difference between 40 clinical reviews and 2 template reviews plus 40 spot-checks.
5. **A shared, live decision log** with a **5-working-day default-approval rule** for staging. Agreed in advance, in writing, today.

---

### R2 — Clinical sign-off bottleneck at a single Medical Director

**The risk.** Dr. Pedraja is a practising clinician. Every service page, every physician profile, the routing table, the red-flag list and the KRISS guardrails all need her signature. She is one person with a day job, and she is also the original KRISS champion — so she is the _most_ engaged stakeholder and the _most_ likely bottleneck simultaneously.
**Mitigation.** (a) Iozera drafts everything; she edits, never authors. (b) Template-level approval, then exception-based review (R1.4). (c) **Batched review sessions** — two 90-minute blocks booked in advance rather than an open-ended email thread. (d) A named clinical deputy with delegated authority for routine profile approvals. (e) Present the KRISS guardrail spec as a **finished written artifact for her to countersign**, not a conversation to have — and frame it as _our deliberate configuration decision for UASHMC_, because KRISS's own public marketing demos include drug guidance ("over-the-counter pain relievers like ibuprofen or acetaminophen can help manage this") and clinical-note generation. If she opens kriss.ai during the meeting and finds that, our "no clinical advice" promise collapses. Get ahead of it.

---

### R3 — Photography: no assets, no shoot date, no releases

**The risk.** Photography is the longest-lead physical dependency and the one most often assumed away. Doctor headshots in particular require catching clinicians across their clinic schedules, and every one needs a signed release. Without photos, Find a Doctor cannot launch — and Find a Doctor is the module that beats ADH.
**Mitigation.** (a) **Book the shoot date in the room, today**, before we discuss anything else about photography. (b) Iozera supplies the shot list, the headshot spec and the release form within two working days. (c) A **half-day in-house headshot station** running across two or three days, set up in a conference room, to catch clinic schedules. (d) Interim spec for physicians who miss it: recent professional photo, plain background, minimum resolution. (e) **Hard rule, stated out loud: no stock photos of people.** For a hospital whose existence a patient cannot yet verify from reputation, stock imagery reads as a hospital that won't show itself. If a shot doesn't exist, we ship the section without it.

---

### R4 — Doctor roster data quality and schedule volatility

**The risk.** Clinic schedules change. A directory with wrong schedules is a complaint generator; a directory with no schedules is a phone-call generator. And missing HMO-per-doctor mappings kill the most valuable facet on the page.
**Mitigation.** (a) A **completeness gate** as a written acceptance criterion — no profile publishes without photo, plain-language specialty, credentials, schedule, HMO list and a 100+ word bio. (b) A **named schedule owner** with a monthly review cadence, agreed today. (c) Publish **typical clinic days** labelled "Schedules may change — confirm when booking" rather than false precision. Honest and stable beats precise and stale. (d) **One canonical provider database** — specialty pages, service pages, homepage and KRISS AI all read the same record, so a correction is made once. (e) An "as of [Month Year]" stamp on the directory plus a "Recently added" strip: a growing roster with dates reads as momentum; a static roster with no dates reads as abandoned.

---

### R5 — Domain and DNS not owned, or credentials unobtainable

**The risk.** The quietest schedule-killer. A launch-ready site sitting on staging because nobody can find who registered the domain, or because the person with registrar access is on leave. Routinely costs a week; occasionally costs three.
**Mitigation.** (a) Resolve on day one — registrar, account holder, DNS access, named as an action item in the meeting. (b) If unowned, **Iozera registers on UASHMC's behalf** with a signed transfer-of-ownership letter, under a registrar account created on a UASHMC-owned mailbox. (c) DNS access verified in **week 2**, not launch week — we test a dummy record early. (d) Bundle the `@uashmc` mailbox provisioning into the same conversation so it happens once.

---

### R6 — HMO, PhilHealth and price content: the highest-harm content on the site

**The risk.** Patient Journey B is the one place where a plausible-sounding answer causes **direct financial harm to a patient** and a reputational hit to a hospital still building trust. "Yes, we accept Maxicare" to someone whose plan excludes the procedure, or whose chosen doctor isn't on Maxicare's accredited list, produces a family with an unexpected bill in the billing queue. A stale published price list is worse than no price list.
**Mitigation.** (a) Publish as **"HMOs we have accreditation agreements with"** — never "HMOs we accept." (b) A visible **"last updated"** date with a named owner, matching established sector practice. (c) The **four caveats on every HMO answer**: plan tier, your doctor, your specific procedure, LOA lead time. (d) Split **Outpatient / Inpatient / Emergency** explicitly — the mechanics genuinely differ and conflating them is how patients get hurt. (e) **KRISS AI hard rule:** it may state whether an agreement exists and what to bring and do next. It may **never** state that a service will be covered, an amount, or that a specific doctor is accredited under a specific plan. Terminal action is always: verify with your HMO **and** UASHMC's HMO desk before admission. (f) No hardcoded PhilHealth peso amounts — case rates are mid-migration to DRG. (g) Billing signs off every money page in writing.

### R7 — Scope creep into patient portal, native booking, EMR/HIS integration

**The risk.** "While we're at it, can it also show lab results / take payments / talk to our HIS?" Every benchmark hospital in the Philippines runs these on separate hostnames or third-party platforms — MakatiMed spans four hostnames plus a Zoho form; Cardinal Santos spans four domains including an external queueing tool. Nobody has integrated them, because it is a different and much larger project.
**Mitigation.** (a) A written Phase 1 boundary in the SOW, with a **Phase 2 candidate list** so nothing is refused, only sequenced. (b) At v1, **deep-link to NowServing** rather than rebuilding booking — this is also the honest framing of what KRISS does, since KRISS's own FAQ states it does not handle scheduling directly and instead links to the provider's portal. (c) Position UASHMC's clean start as the differentiator: **one domain, one design system, one place the patient never has to leave.** Every hand-off elsewhere is a trust drop; we are not adding any.

---

### R8 — KRISS AI expectation gap

**The risk.** UASHMC's patients live on Facebook and Viber. KRISS today is a **website widget only** — no Messenger, no Viber, no WhatsApp, no SMS, no voice. It has **no published language claim**, so Filipino/Taglish must be tested, not assumed. Its shipped capability is Levels 1–2 of its own published six-level roadmap; booking, records, billing and diagnostics are all flagged incomplete. And its public price is **$199/month plus $500 setup** — SMB dental pricing that UASHMC can find in thirty seconds and that will anchor expectations far below a hospital engagement.
**Mitigation.** (a) State the channel scope **in writing, unprompted**, in the first KRISS slide. Channel expansion is scope, not capability. (b) **Test Filipino/Taglish before the meeting** — this will be asked. (c) Pilot on **three journeys only** (HMO, hours/directions, service info) and expand on evidence. (d) Prepare the commercial framing for a hospital engagement — configuration, guardrail authoring, corpus governance, clinical review loop, PIA, ongoing tuning — before anyone finds the dental price. (e) Never claim hospital deployments: kriss.ai's own "Hospital" client category is empty, and its published client list is disabled placeholder data. **Frame UASHMC as the flagship hospital deployment** — which is true, more compelling than a borrowed logo, and something Dr. Bravo may actively want.

---

### R9 — Regulatory and legal review lag

**The risk.** The Privacy Notice, the PIA, the telemedicine claims and the RA 10932 explainer all touch counsel or DOH verification. Several items in the compliance picture are genuinely unsettled: whether the 2020 telemedicine circulars remain in force, whether DOH AO 2021-0008's price-transparency duty reaches websites at all, whether a WCAG duty applies to private entities, and the status of a pending draft NPC circular on PIAs.
**Mitigation.** (a) Start the PIA in **week 3**, in parallel — not as a launch-week discovery. (b) **Phase-boundary the uncertain items**: Phase 1 describes the telemedicine service and how to book it, and publishes no clinical scope claims. (c) Name counsel review as an explicit dependency with a date, owned by UASHMC. (d) **Say the uncertainties out loud at the onsite.** "We will not assert this as settled; it needs your counsel" reads as competence, not hedging — and it is the register Dr. Pedraja and Dr. Directo will respond to. (e) Volunteer the liability position before anyone asks: under NPC Advisory 2024-04, **accountability sits with UASHMC as Personal Information Controller** — it cannot be delegated to Iozera or to the technology. Saying that first is the credibility move, and it converts the KRISS conversation from "cool chatbot" into "governed system with named owners."

---

### R10 — Board / governance review cycle

**The risk.** If the board must ratify scope or spend, and the board meets monthly, an unaligned cycle adds three to four weeks with no work being done.
**Mitigation.** Ask directly today: **can Dr. Bravo approve Phase 1 scope and spend without board ratification?** If yes, get it in writing before we leave. If no, get the board date and build the plan around it — and ask Dr. Directo what the board will want to see, so we prepare that artifact rather than discovering it.

---

### R11 — Too many reviewers, conflicting feedback

**The risk.** Five senior stakeholders reviewing designs independently produces contradictory direction and endless rounds. Marketing wants concern-first navigation; Medical wants departments. Both are legitimate.
**Mitigation.** (a) **One consolidated feedback channel** through the named project owner. Individual emails are logged but not actioned separately. (b) **Two review rounds included; the third is a change order** — stated in the SOW, not sprung later. (c) Resolve the department-vs-concern argument structurally rather than politically: **one canonical service record set, three indexes over it** (by specialty, by concern/body area, A-to-Z). The argument is about navigation, not data — so both sides get their door and neither loses. (d) Decision log entries are closed, dated and not reopened without a change order.

---

### R12 — Front-desk and staff readiness at launch

**The risk.** The site goes live and nobody who answers the phone knows what it says. Patients get one answer online and a different one at the desk, and the site's credibility is undermined by the hospital itself.
**Mitigation.** A 45-minute staff briefing in launch week for front desk, admitting, HMO/billing and the ER station: what the site now answers, where to point patients, and how to report anything wrong. Include a one-page "report a correction" route. This is also the fastest source of genuine content corrections in the first month.

# E) THE 10 DISCOVERY QUESTIONS

Designed to surface **decisions**, not opinions. Every one has a named recipient and a defined use for the answer. Ask them in this order — the authority and ownership questions first, because everything downstream depends on them.

### Q1 — Decision authority and budget

**Ask Dr. Bravo.**

> _"Walk us through how a decision like this actually gets made at UASHMC. Can you approve the Phase 1 scope and spend today, or does it need board ratification — and if it does, when does the board next sit? And is there a date driving this: an anniversary, an accreditation milestone, a census target for a particular quarter?"_

**Why it matters.** Determines whether we are planning an 8-week project or a 12-week one with a 4-week governance gate in the middle. The launch-date question is the one agencies forget to ask and then discover in week six.
**What we do with it.** Sets the Stage 4 approval window and whether the fast path is even achievable. If a hard date exists, it selects the path for us rather than us presenting a choice.

### Q2 — What success looks like

**Ask Dr. Bravo, with Harvey.**

> _"Twelve months after this site is live, what has changed that makes you say it was worth it? Is it census, HMO-patient mix, inbound calls that arrive already informed, doctor recruitment, or something else? And what's the six-month version of that?"_

**Why it matters.** A brand-new hospital building awareness could reasonably optimise for census, for referral-source diversification, for HMO patient share, or for physician recruitment — and those four produce **materially different homepages.** Nobody has told us which. Asking makes the project accountable rather than decorative.
**What we do with it.** Becomes the measurement plan, the analytics configuration, the homepage priority order, and the agenda of the quarterly business review. It also gives us the one slide the board will actually remember.

### Q3 — Domain and email ownership

Can we agree today to stand up hospital-domain mailboxes, and who has the authority to make that happen?"_

**Why it matters.** The quietest schedule-killer (R5), and simultaneously the cheapest large credibility win available. Framing it as a data-security remediation item rather than a branding preference makes it Tere's action item this week instead of a Phase 2 nice-to-have.
**What we do with it.** Unblocks launch mechanics on day one, resolves a live privacy exposure, and gives Tere a visible win before any design exists.

**Why it matters.** This is the mitigation for the #1 risk, and it must be settled in the room. A project with five stakeholders and no owner does not slip; it stalls. Naming the post-launch steward _at kickoff_ rather than at launch is what prevents the site going stale in ninety days.

**Why it matters.** A genuine business decision with commercial consequences, correctly theirs and not ours. Note honestly that DOH AO 2021-0008's price-transparency duty speaks to **physical conspicuous areas**; whether it reaches websites is unsettled, so online transparency is a **differentiator, not an obligation.** Presenting it as a choice with a cost attached rather than a fait accompli is what makes it a real decision for Harvey to own.

Phrased as asks, in the order to make them. Each is small, specific, and answerable on the day. Get these nine and the project starts on Monday.

### The five that must not be left unresolved

**1. Name the project owner.**

> _"Who is our one person at UASHMC — the one who consolidates feedback and can say yes? Can we write the name down now?"_

**2. Put the standing call in the calendar, in the room.**

> _"Thirty minutes, same day and time every week, starting next week. Can we send the invite before we leave?"_
> _(Thirty, not sixty. Short enough that it never gets cancelled.)_

**3. Book the photo shoot date.**

> _"We'll send the shot list and the headshot spec within two days. What date works for the facility shoot, and can we run a two- or three-day headshot station to catch clinic schedules?"_

**4. Settle the domain and the email migration.**

> _"Which domain, who holds the registrar account, and do we have approval to stand up `@uashmc` mailboxes and retire the Gmail address from the public contact page — with 90 days of forwarding so nothing is lost?"_

**5. Commit to Content Pack #1 in ten working days.**

> _"We'll send five pre-formatted spreadsheets in two days: doctors, services, HMOs, departments, rooms — with example rows filled in. Can we agree a ten-working-day return, and can [named owner] chase it?"_

### The four that make the next stage frictionless

**6. Approve the physician profile template in principle, today.**

> _"Dr. Pedraja — here's the draft template. If you can mark it up today, every subsequent profile is an exception review rather than a fresh approval. That single decision is worth two to three weeks."_

**7. Get the LTO, or a written commitment to it with a date.**

> _"We can't publish a licensing block or a service list without it, and it's the strongest single credibility asset you have. Who can pull it, and by when?"_

**8. Agree the path: fast or full.**

> _"Eight weeks to a site that beats every hospital in Antipolo, or seventeen to the full catalogue. Our recommendation is to launch the eight-week version and fund the enrichment at launch — because the 'first Philippine hospital with a real on-site assistant' claim has a shelf life, and the competitor four kilometres down Marcos Highway has a website that isn't responding today. Which do you want?"_

**9. Agree the working rules, in writing, before anyone forgets them.**

> _"Four rules that protect your schedule and ours: one consolidated feedback channel through [owner]. Two design review rounds included, a third is a change order. A five-working-day default approval for staging, so build never stalls waiting on a signature. And a shared decision log that neither side gets to relitigate. Can we adopt those?"_

### Leave behind, in writing, on the day

- The five content-request spreadsheets, pre-formatted with example rows
- The physician media release form, including the KRISS AI data-use clause
- The photography shot list and headshot spec
- The draft physician profile template
- The **KRISS AI guardrail spec** — unprompted, before anyone asks for it
- The live decision log, with the day's decisions already entered and shared

### The one line to close on

> _"Nothing on this list is a design decision. Every one of them is a commitment about who does what, by when. Give us those nine and you have the best hospital website in Rizal in eight weeks — and the first real patient assistant on any Philippine hospital site in twelve."_
