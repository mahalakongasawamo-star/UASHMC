# Three Patient Journeys (+ One Bonus)

## Website only vs. Website + KRISS AI

_Prepared for the UASHMC onsite — website workstream, Iozera.ai_

---

## How to read these

Each journey is shown three ways, not two, because **the honest baseline is not "website only" — it is "no website," which is where UASHMC is today.**

1. **TODAY** — the real path a patient walks right now, with steps counted and time elapsed.
2. **WEBSITE ONLY** — what a well-built site fixes, and **where it still fails.** We show the failures on purpose.
3. **WEBSITE + KRISS AI** — the same journey as an actual conversation.

### Conventions used below

| Marker               | Meaning                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `[TO SUPPLY: …]`     | A hospital fact we deliberately have **not** invented. Every one of these is a content-gathering ask for Monday. Do not let a placeholder become a published number. |
| _Illustrative names_ | Doctor names, and all step/time counts, are illustrative and clearly labelled. Real names come from the roster UASHMC supplies.                                      |
| Source lines         | Every KRISS answer ends with the approved page it came from. That line is not decoration — it is the compliance mechanism.                                           |

### The KRISS voice, defined before we use it

Calm. Plain. Institutional. Reading age ~11. **No exclamation marks, no emoji, no mascot, no "Hi there! 👋".** Answers in the language the patient wrote in, including Taglish. Never says "you may have," "this sounds like," or "I recommend." Always names one concrete next step. Always cites the hospital page it drew from.

> **Quiet is a trust signal.** A hospital assistant that behaves like a sales popup undermines the hospital. KRISS does not auto-open, does not chime, does not fake an unread badge.

### Honesty box — what is true about KRISS today

So nobody in the room overclaims and nobody gets caught:

- KRISS is a **website widget.** Phase 1 does not put it inside Facebook Messenger, Viber, or SMS. Those are where UASHMC's patients live today, which is exactly why the website has to become the place they land.
- **KRISS does not book appointments.** It deep-links into NowServing.ph — which UASHMC already runs. This is a feature, not a gap: informing and booking stay in separate, auditable systems.
- KRISS answers from **hospital-approved content only.** It is not a medical knowledge engine. Its ceiling is exactly what UASHMC publishes and approves — which is why content gathering is the critical path, not design.
- The dialogues below are **the approved configuration we are proposing**, written to spec. They are not transcripts of a live system.

# JOURNEY A — "Sino ang dapat makita ng tatay ko?"

### _The patient who does not know the word "cardiologist"_

## The person

**Maricel Dizon, 41.** Registrar at a private school in Cogeo. Lives in a two-storey house off Marcos Highway in Mambugan with her husband, two kids, and her father.

Her father, **Tatay Ben, 68**, has been stopping halfway up their stairs to catch his breath. At the barangay health centre last week his BP read high — she remembers "one-sixty something." He shrugged it off. She has not.

**Device:** Samsung Galaxy A-series, hairline crack across the top third of the screen. Mobile data on a weekly promo, so she is mildly data-conscious. Facebook is her internet.

**Time:** Tuesday, 9:47 PM. Kids finally down. She is lying on her side in bed, phone six inches from her face, one thumb.

**Emotional state:** Not panicking. _Tired and quietly frightened._ She is the family's designated person-who-handles-things, and she has been putting this off for eight days because she does not know where to start. She does not use the word "cardiologist." She thinks _"heart doctor,"_ and she is not certain that is even the right one.

### Where the website alone still fails her

She now knows _who_. She still does not know:

- **"Kailangan ba ng referral?"** She has never brought her father to a private hospital. She does not know if she can just show up.
- **"May senior discount ba sa consult?"** This is a real cost question and she is on one income.
- And the thing she has not typed anywhere yet: **her father also said his chest hurts when he walks.** She does not know if that changes anything, and there is no one to ask at 9:55 PM.

She bookmarks the page. She does not book. She intends to call tomorrow.

**Honest assessment: the website converted her from "no idea" to "informed but stalled." That is a large improvement and an incomplete outcome.** Realistically, a meaningful share of stalled evening visitors never call.

**The clinical line for Dr. Pedraja:** the only reason the chest-pain disclosure ever surfaced is that there was somewhere to type it. A website cannot receive that sentence. And when it received it, the system did not attempt to be a doctor.

## 5. What UASHMC must supply for Journey A

1. Complete cardiology and internal medicine roster: photo, PRC licence, board fellowship spelled out, sub-specialty, **clinic days and hours with a named owner for updates**, room number, languages, 100+ word bio.
2. **Medical Director-approved concern→specialty routing table** (~100–200 rows) — the artefact that makes "pahingal at mataas ang BP" resolve to Cardiology/Internal Medicine without any AI inference. Signed, dated, version-controlled, owned by Dr. Pedraja.
3. **Red-flag vocabulary list**, clinically approved, that triggers the emergency interrupt. Reviewed on a stated cadence, with every trigger logged for the Medical Director.
4. Confirmed **ER hours and ER direct line**, and confirmation of the ER entrance vs main entrance.
5. Referral policy per clinic. Senior/PWD benefits page. Elevator and PWD parking locations.
6. NowServing profile URLs per doctor, so the deep-link lands on the right doctor and not a search page.

Two weeks ago an ultrasound at a diagnostic centre in Marikina found gallstones. The doctor said the word **"cholecystectomy"** and told him to have it done before it becomes an emergency. He has **Maxicare** through work.

**Device:** iPhone SE, work-issued. On the production floor, on his 15-minute break, brightness down.

**Time:** Wednesday, 1:20 AM. He is mid-shift. He will get home at 7:30 AM, sleep until 4 PM, and go back to work.

**Emotional state:** Not scared of surgery. **Scared of the bill, and scared of the LOA runaround** — he went through it two years ago for his mother and remembers being sent between three desks. What he actually wants to know is whether he will be blindsided.
